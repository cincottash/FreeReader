FreeReader Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 28, 2026

FreeReader Home Terms of Service

FreeReader is a free e-book reader for Android, Windows and Linux, made by cincottash. This policy explains what happens to your information when you use it. In short: FreeReader has no ads, no analytics and no tracking, it needs no account, and its developer receives none of your data. What you read and do stays on your devices, and, only if you turn Sync on, in your own Google Drive.

Information stored on your device

  • The books you add (FreeReader keeps its own copy of each on the phone), your reading positions, highlights, notes, bookmarks, your Word List and flashcard progress, reading statistics and your settings.
  • These are kept in FreeReader's private storage on your device. On Android, the system's own backup to your Google Account may include them if you have device backup turned on.
  • API keys you enter (Google Gemini) and the Google sign-in token used by Sync are kept in the system's protected key store (Android Keystore, Windows Credential Manager, or the Secret Service on Linux), never in a plain file.

Google Drive Sync (optional)

Sync keeps your library the same on all your devices. It is off until you choose Sign In With Google in FreeReader's settings. When you sign in:

  • What FreeReader accesses: only its own hidden app data folder in your Google Drive (Google's scope https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata). FreeReader cannot see, open or change any other file in your Drive. It also reads your Google Account's name and email address from Google Drive, only to show in Settings which account is signed in; that stays on your device.
  • What FreeReader stores there: one sync file per device (your reading positions, highlights, notes, bookmarks, Word List, flashcard progress and review history, reading statistics, collections, and settings such as text size and theme), copies of the book files in your library with their covers, and saved translations. API keys and passwords are never synced.
  • How it is used: only to show the same books and the same progress on your other devices signed in to the same Google Account. The data goes directly between your devices and Google Drive over an encrypted connection (HTTPS). There is no FreeReader server: the developer never receives, sees or stores your data, and it is never sold, shared with anyone else, used for advertising, or used to train AI models.
  • Turning it off and deleting it: Sign Out in FreeReader's settings stops Sync on that device and removes its sign-in token. To delete everything FreeReader stored in your Drive, open Google Drive on the web, Settings, Manage Apps, FreeReader, Options, Delete Hidden App Data. You can also remove FreeReader's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • The files count toward your Google Drive storage, and Google keeps them under Google's Privacy Policy.

FreeReader's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Information sent over the internet, only when you use a feature

  • Translations by Google Gemini (optional). Gemini is used only after you enter your own Gemini API key. Then, when you look up a word or passage, FreeReader sends the selected text and the sentence around it to Google's Gemini service, which returns the translation. Nothing else from your books is sent. Google handles that text under its own terms (Gemini API terms); on Gemini's free tier Google may use it to improve its products. Without a key, no text is sent.
  • LM Studio (optional). If you set up LM Studio, a program on your own computer, the selected text is sent to that computer on your own network, and nowhere else.
  • Dictionary downloads (optional). When you choose Download in Settings, FreeReader downloads the dictionary file from FreeReader's page on GitHub. GitHub sees the request like any web download (your IP address); nothing else is sent.
  • Links you open. Links such as Get a Free Key, Full Definition (Wiktionary) and Report open your web browser. Report opens a pre-filled issue on FreeReader's GitHub page with the text being reported; nothing is sent unless you submit it there, and what you submit is public.

Android permissions

  • Internet and network state: for Sync, Gemini, LM Studio and dictionary downloads, only when you use them.
  • Files: books are chosen with the system's file picker; FreeReader reads only the files you pick.

Children

FreeReader is a general-audience reading app and isn't directed at children. It collects no personal information from anyone.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will be on this page with a new date. Questions or requests: open an issue at github.com/cincottash/FreeReader/issues.
